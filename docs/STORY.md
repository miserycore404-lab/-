# КОРДОН / CORDON — story bible

> *Зима, когда кончилась страна.* — *The winter the country ended.*

## Premise

December 1991. Moscow Oblast, a forest cordon (a forester's outpost) at the
101st kilometre of the Savyolovsky suburban line. The Soviet Union has days
left to live.

**Arkady Lvovich Belov**, 56, drove trains on the Moscow Metro's Circle Line for
24 years: 28 minutes a lap, the same stations forever. He has answered a
newspaper ad for a watchman's post at the cordon where his father, the forester
**Lev Matveyevich**, lived and fed bullfinches for forty winters.

In his inside pocket is a letter from his son **Kostya**, killed near Kandahar
in November 1988, three months before the withdrawal. It arrived two weeks
after the sealed-coffin funeral. Arkady hid it from his wife **Galya** and has
never opened it. In October 1991 Galya left for Leningrad (now St. Petersburg)
and told him, *"You never cried, Arkasha. Not once."*

The only other voice in the white silence belongs to **Zoya Fyodorovna**, the
forestry dispatcher. She is call sign *Birch*, he is *Bullfinch*. She is
dry-witted and a chain-smoking single mother. She grew up in the nearby
village, following old Belov around like Grandfather Frost.

## Motifs

- **The circle.** The Metro ring, the routine, the grief he circles and never
  enters. The prologue is drawn as stations on the Circle Line; the last
  choice breaks off the ring onto a branch line to "101 km".
- **Doorways.** Arkady always stands in doorways (his father's journal says
  so). The first door in the game is frozen shut.
- **Bullfinches.** They whistle softly, "as if apologising". Red on white, the
  only warm accent in the palette. Kostya's P.S. promises to test his father's
  whistle; the game ends with Arkady whistling and the birds answering.
- **Silence as a choice.** Every radio conversation lets you say nothing, and
  the story listens to that silence too.

## Structure

| Part | Date | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| Prologue | 1965–1991 | A life as Circle Line stations, with choices that set flags (how he met Galya, the promise to feed the birds, hiding the letter). |
| Ch. 1 *Bullfinch* | Fri 20 Dec, dusk | The train leaves him at the platform. He walks to the cordon, forces the frozen door, lights the lamp and the stove, meets Zoya on the radio, makes Indian instant coffee in his father's railway tea-glass holder, and writes the first log entry. |
| Ch. 2 *Kilometre 101* | Mon 23 Dec, frost | He feeds the bullfinches (keeping or breaking the 1979 promise), checks the catenary as a train thunders past, and tells Zoya about Kostya or lies to her. Zoya sends him to an old birch by the pond, where he finds "K.B. 1977" carved in the bark. |
| Ch. 3 *The Flag* | Wed 25 Dec, night | Radio Mayak broadcasts Gorbachev's resignation and the lowering of the flag. Zoya, losing her job on 1 January, asks why he really came. He reads the letter. Zoya patches the forestry radio into a telephone line to Leningrad, and he calls Galya. |
| Epilogue | Thu 26 Dec, morning | The first morning without the USSR. He whistles to the bullfinches at the feeder. |

## Kostya's letter (excerpt)

> Dad, I know you can't talk about things. You spent your whole life going
> round in a circle and keeping everything inside, like in a tunnel. That's all
> right. I heard it all anyway…
>
> …don't go quiet on Mum. When you're silent she thinks she's alone. Say
> something to her. Anything. Promise.
